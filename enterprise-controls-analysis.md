# Wells Fargo: Enterprise Control Analysis

**Experience summary | Corporate Risk | Data-led transformation discovery**

**Scope and output:** Led analysis of **149,464 controls across seven lines of business**, organized into **40 clusters and 24,622 sub-clusters** to expose redundancy candidates and automation opportunities.

## Business problem

A large enterprise control inventory made it difficult to see repeated work, inconsistent descriptions and opportunities to simplify or automate. Local views could miss patterns across business units.

## My contribution

I led analysis of the enterprise Master Control List, used clustering and text analysis to group related controls, and used Power BI reporting to make the patterns easier to examine. I partnered with business and transformation teams on automation opportunities carried through parallel change initiatives.

This moved the conversation from individual records to enterprise patterns and a more focused set of candidates for business review.

## Outcome and boundaries

The analysis produced the **40-cluster / 24,622-sub-cluster** structure and surfaced candidates for simplification and bot automation. The inventory size and group counts are delivered analytical outputs. They do not establish how many controls were redundant, removed or automated, or the financial benefit realized.

## How I would qualify candidates for action

The reusable decision approach below separates similarity from a safe change decision:

| Decision | Evidence required | Accountable partner |
|---|---|---|
| Are the controls substantively equivalent? | Purpose, obligation, process, population, frequency and evidence requirements | Control/business owner |
| Can the work be standardized? | Common operating need and documented local differences | Process owner |
| Is automation appropriate? | Stable rules, usable data, exception path and support owner | Business and technology owners |
| Can a control be changed or retired? | Approved coverage assessment, replacement design and required governance | Authorized control/risk owner |
| Was value realized? | Implemented scope, measured effort/quality change and accepted benefit | Process owner and Finance |

Similar wording is a review signal; it is not sufficient authority to remove a control. A future-state design needs clear ownership, evidence and monitoring.

# Wells Fargo: Roadmap, Delivery and Control Governance

**Experience summary | Corporate Risk product leadership**

**Reported delivery:** 60+ releases in 2024 and 25+ change outputs with 100% on-time delivery in 2025, supported by a six-quarter roadmap tied to OKRs.

## From strategy to execution

I connected the roadmap to business outcomes and used delivery planning, risk/dependency visibility and review routines to coordinate work across business and technology partners. The leadership task was to make priorities, ownership and dependencies clear enough for teams to act.

The roadmap provided a sequence for delivery. OKRs supplied the outcome context; releases and change outputs showed execution progress. A release count is useful evidence of delivery, while operational measures determine whether the change improved the business.

## Controls by design and remediation

- Established owners for **100% of product risks**, supported by monthly reviews and risk/dependency dashboards.
- Analyzed approximately **3,000 defects and 79 issues**, identifying **30+ process and control weaknesses** for remediation.
- Implemented **10+ Jira automation rules**, reducing manual record errors by **up to 95%** in the affected work.

These results describe product governance, issue analysis and record-quality improvement. Weaknesses identified are not automatically weaknesses remediated; closure requires evidence and the accountable owner's acceptance.

## Executive communication pattern

For an executive update, I use a short decision structure:

1. **Outcome:** What business result are we trying to achieve?
2. **Status:** What changed against the baseline, commitment or milestone?
3. **Decision:** What needs approval, prioritization or escalation now?
4. **Tradeoff:** What happens to value, timing, cost or risk under each option?
5. **Accountability:** Who owns the action and when will we review it?

The [illustrative executive brief](https://github.com/ddjfam/process-engineering-playbook/blob/main/executive-decision-brief.md) shows this structure in a transformation investment decision.

## Distributed delivery pattern

For a new cross-location program, I would establish one accountable process owner, named local champions, a shared decision log and a regular delivery/benefits review. Local exceptions need a reason, an owner and a review date. The [CoE charter](https://github.com/ddjfam/process-engineering-playbook/blob/main/coe-charter-and-governance.md) provides a proposed decision-rights model.
